# Three content opportunity briefs
For a hypothetical production or streaming company. Evidence is launch attention (English-Wikipedia pageviews, days 0–27), not viewership. It covers only titles that had a Wikipedia article at release (55% of scripted titles) and under-represents regional-language audiences. Genre means the first genre listed for a title. Each brief states its evidence grade; 23 language–genre cells were compared, so single-cell results near p = 0.05 are weak on their own.

## Brief 1: Reallocate Hindi drama slots — evidence: strong
- **Observation.** Since 2020, titles billed first as drama are 53 of 247 measured Hindi releases (21%) and draw 0.30 times the Hindi median launch attention (95% CI 0.16–0.69; Mann-Whitney p < 0.001, which survives correction for 23 comparisons). The since-2023 cut gives 0.21 (CI 0.12–0.89). Across languages, with controls, action, horror and crime each draw about twice a drama.
- **Opportunity.** Stop commissioning Hindi dramas that have no sharper genre frame, known cast or event hook, and move those slots to projects that have one.
- **Test.** Before greenlight, a Hindi drama should show which of the three levers it has. Track launch attention for the next ten by whether they had one.
- **Risk.** "Drama" here is partly a label for titles nobody described more specifically. Attention is not completion; quiet dramas may retain subscribers in ways this proxy cannot see.

## Brief 2: Hindi action for streaming — evidence: moderate
- **Observation.** Since 2020, 21 measured Hindi action titles (9% of Hindi supply) have 1.51 times the Hindi median launch attention (CI 1.05–3.79, p = 0.016). Across all languages, with platform, format, cast and year controlled, action draws 2.4 times a drama (CI 1.5–3.8, p < 0.001).
- **Opportunity.** A thinly supplied genre whose launches draw above-median attention on two separate cuts of the data.
- **Test.** Two mid-budget Hindi action titles, with success defined in advance as exceeding the Hindi median launch attention.
- **Risk.** The cell-level p-value does not survive a multiple-comparison correction by itself. Action is expensive, and several high-attention action titles are star vehicles; cast prominence is controlled in the regression but not in the cell comparison.

## Brief 3: Tamil features for national platforms — evidence: suggestive only
- **Observation.** With controls, Tamil titles are not distinguishable from Hindi (0.93 times, CI 0.63–1.38). That is an absence of a detectable gap, not proof of parity. Tamil films have a median of 191,000 pageviews against 234,000 for Hindi films, on 46 versus 154 measured titles. Tamil crime is at 1.70 times the Tamil median (12 titles, CI 0.42–4.48).
- **Opportunity.** If the gap is as small as the central estimate suggests, Tamil features offer near-Hindi launch attention from a much smaller slate.
- **Test.** Commission two or three Tamil crime or action features with pan-India dubbing and compare launch attention with the Hindi film median.
- **Risk.** Wide intervals throughout; nothing at genre level is statistically distinguishable for Tamil. Tamil comedy looks weak (0.22, nine titles, CI 0.06–0.97) but that is tentative too.
